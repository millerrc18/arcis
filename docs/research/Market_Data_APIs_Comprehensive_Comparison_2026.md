# Market Data APIs for an AI-Powered Equity and Options Trading System in 2026

## Scope, evaluation criteria, and workload model

This research targets a practical “AI trading system” workload: daily OHLCV for ~325 U.S. large-cap equities plus end-of-day (EOD) options chains with Greeks and implied volatility (IV), augmented with fundamentals, news/sentiment, and a small set of alternative datasets (free or <$100/month). The emphasis is on feasibility under your two-phase budget plan: $0–$200/month initially, with a clear upgrade path toward ~$500/month in Phase 3.

A realistic baseline request volume (worst case: **1 request per symbol**) for daily OHLCV is about **325 requests/day** and **~6,800 requests/month** (assuming ~21 trading days). Options chains can be far more data-intensive; if you pull “full chain by expiration” you can multiply calls quickly. As a result, the “best” provider for options often isn’t simply the cheapest, but the one that gives you (a) **precomputed Greeks/IV** and (b) **efficient chain retrieval** (snapshots / bulk endpoints) so you don’t explode rate limits or latency.

## Fundamental data APIs for estimates, ratings, and SEC filings

Your fundamental requirements—earnings estimates, analyst ratings, SEC filings—are the most heterogeneous. “Fundamentals” can mean:
- **reported financials** (10-Q/10-K statements),
- **consensus estimates and revisions** (EPS/sales/EBITDA estimates, changes over time),
- **analyst ratings and price targets**,
- **filings metadata + document retrieval**.

A common “serious” architecture is: **(1) vendor API for structured estimates/ratings + standardized financial statements** + **(2) direct EDGAR ingestion for primary-source filings** (for auditability and to fill gaps).

## News and sentiment APIs

News is where “AI-powered trading system” teams often overspend, because it’s tempting to ingest everything. For a 325-stock universe, the most cost-effective pattern is:
1) build a **ticker-filtered feed** (only store articles that map to your symbols),
2) store only the fields you actually model (timestamp, source reliability info, title/body, tickers, entities),
3) run your own downstream embeddings/sentiment to avoid being locked into one vendor’s scoring rubric.

## Cross-cutting gotchas you should design around

- **EDGAR ingestion must be rate-controlled**: SEC states automated requests should not exceed 10 requests/second. Build a centralized throttling layer and add caching/persistence so you never re-download unnecessarily. citeturn26search7turn26search3  
- **Brokerage APIs are not historical options databases**: Tradier explicitly limits historical options data for expired contracts, which can silently break backtests if you assume you can query past chains later. citeturn23search18  
- **Options Greeks/IV are not “raw truth”**: even where Greeks/IV are provided, they are model-derived and can vary by methodology; where they are not provided (Databento), you must pick and maintain the model yourself. citeturn19search7turn19search3  
- **Credits/call-currency systems require capacity planning**: Twelve Data’s “credits/min” vary by endpoint weight, and EODHD distinguishes “requests/min” from “API calls/day” as currency. Your job scheduler should model consumption, not just “number of HTTP calls.” citeturn10view0turn17view0  
- **Free “alternative data” is often fragile**: pytrends is notoriously prone to 429s and blocking. Treat it like an opportunistic feature generator with backoff and caching, not a guaranteed feed. citeturn28search0turn28search4turn28search12
