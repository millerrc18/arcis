# From $100 to Registered Fund: The Solo Quant's Complete Roadmap

**A systematic equity trader running an AI-powered strategy on S&P 100 stocks can realistically reach $3M AUM within 3–5 years, but the path demands approximately $50K–$75K in startup costs, a minimum 12–24 month audited live track record, and $10M+ AUM before the fund economics support full-time management.** The critical insight: FundSeeder (recently revitalized under RQSI in 2025) is the single most accessible on-ramp for a solo operator with sub-$100K capital, while the friends-and-family fund structure under Section 3(c)(1) and Regulation D Rule 506(b) provides the legal framework for early capital aggregation. Registration as an RIA — not CPO — is correct for an equity-only, no-derivatives strategy, and Exempt Reporting Adviser status under Section 203(m) eliminates full SEC registration until AUM exceeds $150M.

This roadmap covers every operational, legal, and economic dimension of the journey from Halcyon Lab's current $100 live capital position through Wyoming LLC formation, incubator participation, and ultimately registered fund status at $3M AUM base case.

**Paper trading records carry virtually zero weight with institutional allocators.** The SEC's Marketing Rule adopting release states that hypothetical performance poses "a high risk of misleading investors since, in many cases, they may be readily optimized through hindsight." ## Phase 2: Incubator fund formation and friends-and-family capital (Months 12–30)

### Forming the incubator fund

After 12+ months of verified live track record with strong risk-adjusted performance, form a proper fund vehicle. An **incubator fund** is a self-funded structure where the manager trades exclusively their own capital within a legal fund entity, creating an auditable, GIPS-eligible track record. No outside investors, no PPM required, minimal compliance burden.

**Structure:** Form a Delaware LLC (the fund) managed by Halcyon Lab LLC (Wyoming, the manager). A Delaware LLC fund costs ~$300 to form and $300/year in franchise tax. The single-entity approach is simpler and cheaper than the traditional LP/GP structure, and is perfectly adequate for early-stage and friends-and-family rounds. Convert to Delaware LP later only if institutional investors require it.

**Incubator fund cost: $5,000–$15,000/year** (entity maintenance, basic legal, trading costs). No audit required during the self-funded incubator phase.

### Transitioning to friends-and-family capital

When the track record reaches 12–18 months with compelling metrics, begin accepting outside capital. This requires proper legal documentation and regulatory compliance.

**Investment Company Act exemption: Section 3(c)(1).** This exempts the fund from registering as an investment company provided it has 100 or fewer beneficial owners and does not make a public offering. This is the standard for early-stage funds — simpler than Section 3(c)(7), which requires all investors to be qualified purchasers ($5M+ in investments).

**Securities offering exemption: Regulation D, Rule 506(b).** This is the correct choice for friends-and-family because it allows raising unlimited capital from accredited investors plus up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors, requires only pre-existing relationships (which friends and family inherently satisfy), permits self-certification of accreditation (no third-party verification needed), and creates "covered securities" that preempt state registration. File Form D with the SEC within 15 days of first sale (no filing fee). State blue sky notice filings are required in each state where investors reside, typically $100–$1,200 per state. New York requires filing before the first sale; Florida requires no filing at all.

### RIA registration: when and what type

**For a systematic equity trader with no leverage, no derivatives, no commodities, and no short selling, RIA registration is correct — not CPO/CTA.** CPO and CTA registration are governed by the CFTC and apply only when trading futures, options on futures, swaps, or retail forex. Pure equity trading falls entirely under SEC jurisdiction via the Investment Advisers Act of 1940.

Registration triggers when all three prongs are met: providing advice about securities, being in the business of doing so, and receiving compensation. Accepting management/performance fees from outside investors triggers this requirement.

**Exempt Reporting Adviser (ERA) status under Section 203(m) is the optimal starting position.** The Private Fund Adviser Exemption allows an adviser who solely advises private funds with less than $150M total AUM to avoid full SEC registration while filing a truncated Form ADV (Part 1A only, no brochure requirement). ERA filing costs $150/year through IARD. This eliminates the need for a full compliance program until AUM grows substantially.

At full AUM thresholds: under $25M registers with the state; $25M–$110M generally remains state-registered; above $110M must register with the SEC within 90 days of fiscal year-end.

**If the system eventually trades equity options (stock options, not futures options), no change in registration is required.** Exchange-traded equity options are SEC-regulated securities, not CFTC-regulated commodity interests. Only options on futures would potentially trigger CFTC considerations, and even then, CFTC Rule 4.13(a)(3) provides a de minimis exemption if commodity interest positions remain below 5% of NAV.

## Phase 3: Scaling to $1M–$3M AUM and the FundSeeder/seeder pathway (Months 24–48)

### The incubator and seeder landscape in 2025–2026

The term "incubator" in fund management refers to a self-funded fund vehicle for track record building — not an external program. **Seeders** provide institutional capital in exchange for revenue share or equity. **Accelerators** provide capital plus operational infrastructure plus mentoring. The landscape for a sub-$100K solo operator is narrower than commonly assumed.

**FundSeeder/RQSI remains the most accessible path.** No minimum AUM, free platform, broker-verified performance, and the possibility of institutional seeding. However, the selection rate is approximately **0.07%** (14 seeded out of 20,000 registered), and featured seeded traders had 4+ years of tracked performance.

**Kottke Associates ceased independent operations in 2016** when HTG Capital Partners acquired its businesses. No longer an option. The entity referred to as "ERA" (Emerging Research Associates) as a hedge fund incubator could not be verified through extensive searching — no website, no track record requirements, no fee structure found. This may be defunct, may use a different name, or may be a private entity without public presence.

**Major institutional seeders are not accessible at this stage.** Blackstone Strategic Alliance Fund offers $250M–$450M per deal to managers spinning out of major firms. Lighthouse Investment Partners seeds at $150M+ to experienced portfolio managers. GCM Grosvenor targets funds of $50M–$400M. Investcorp explicitly states "we are seeders, not incubators" and requires pedigree from established firms. These programs target a fundamentally different profile.

**First-loss capital platforms become relevant at $500K+ personal capital.** Ashton Global requires the manager to contribute 10% of total allocation as first-loss capital (e.g., $500K for a $5M allocation) and pays 50–55% of profits. Falcon Investment Management operates a similar model requiring average annual returns of 10–15%+ with low drawdowns.

### Typical seeder deal terms

Revenue share arrangements typically involve **15–25% of gross top-line revenues** (management fees plus performance allocations) or **10–25% of management company equity**. Lock-up periods run 2–3 years hard lock. Loss triggers allow seeder withdrawal at 15–20% performance decline. The timeline from first contact to capital allocation runs 6–12+ months for institutional seeders, with GCM Grosvenor noting that "top-tier managers have spent an average of over two years raising their inaugural fund."

### The minimum viable track record

Track record requirements vary dramatically by allocator tier:

**Family offices and high-net-worth individuals (lowest bar):** 6–12 months live, sometimes investing at launch with strong conviction. AUM as low as $1M–$5M. Backtested results accepted as supplement to live performance. The fastest path to initial capital.

**Fund of funds and emerging manager programs:** 1–3 years live, audited. AUM typically $10M–$100M. Independent fund administrator is non-negotiable — **73% of allocators view absent independent administration as an immediate red flag**. Sharpe ratio ≥1.0 expected over available track record.

**Pensions and endowments (highest bar):** 3–5 years audited through multiple market conditions. AUM generally $100M+. GIPS compliance strongly preferred (75% of consultants expect it). Full institutional infrastructure required. Consultant relationships serve as gatekeepers; the due diligence process runs 12–24+ months.

**The metrics that matter most:** Sharpe ratio of **1.0+ is the minimum credible threshold** (S&P 500 long-term Sharpe is ~0.49); **1.5–2.0 is competitive** for professional managers. Maximum drawdown should stay under **15–20%** for moderate-volatility strategies. Sortino ratio >2.0 is preferred by 73% of institutional investors. For a systematic pullback-in-uptrend strategy on S&P 100 stocks, a Sharpe above 1.5 with max drawdown under 10% would be highly competitive.

### SEC Marketing Rule compliance for showing performance

The SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1, effective November 2022) permits showing hypothetical and backtested performance but imposes strict conditions. Written policies must ensure hypothetical performance is relevant to the audience's financial situation. Sufficient disclosure of criteria, assumptions, risks, and limitations is mandatory. **Hypothetical performance must never appear on a publicly accessible website** — this is the #1 enforcement trigger. Distribution must be limited to qualified purchasers or clients in one-on-one settings (except in response to unsolicited requests or to private fund investors). A December 2025 SEC Risk Alert highlighted common deficiencies, and updated FAQs from March 2025 and January 2026 provide further guidance on extracted performance and model fees.

### GIPS compliance timing

GIPS is voluntary but increasingly expected. Over 1,600 organizations claim compliance globally, including all top 25 asset managers. Firms in existence fewer than 5 years must present GIPS-compliant performance since inception. Early compliance signals credibility — research shows smaller, less-established funds are actually **more likely** to be GIPS compliant, using it as a certification signal. Initial GIPS setup costs **$15,000–$50,000**; annual verification runs **$10,000–$30,000** for small funds. ACA Group is the dominant provider with 80+ professionals and 1,100+ clients.

## Phase 4: Full fund operations and path to $3M+ AUM (Months 36–60)

### One-person fund compliance architecture

Running a registered fund as a sole operator requires wearing every hat. Here is the complete compliance infrastructure:

**Chief Compliance Officer:** The sole principal can and should serve as CCO — only ~3% of firms under $100M AUM employ a dedicated CCO. The SEC requires the CCO to be "competent and knowledgeable regarding the Advisers Act" and empowered with full authority. Personal liability is real: the SEC has sanctioned individual CCOs for compliance failures. An outsourced compliance consultant handles the heavy lifting while the principal remains the named CCO on Form ADV.

**Code of Ethics (Rule 204A-1):** Must include standards of business conduct, compliance with federal securities laws, personal securities transaction reporting, violation reporting procedures, and written acknowledgment by supervised persons. **Critical exception for one-person funds:** Section 204A-1(d) eliminates the requirement to submit reports to yourself or pre-approve your own trades — but you must still maintain records of all holdings and transactions.

**Compliance manual:** Must cover portfolio management, personal trading, disclosure accuracy, asset safeguarding, recordkeeping, marketing, valuation, cybersecurity, business continuity, and conflict management. Templates are available (RIA Express at $995 for DIY; consultant-prepared at $3,500–$15,000) but the SEC warns against unmodified boilerplate — policies must be "reasonably designed" for your specific operations.

**Privacy policy (Regulation S-P):** The 2024 amendments impose new requirements with a compliance deadline of **June 3, 2026 for smaller RIAs**. New mandates include a written Incident Response Program, service provider breach notification within 72 hours, customer notification of compromised information, and 6-year record retention. For a solo operator, the IRP must designate a backup contact (outside attorney or consultant) since one person handles all detection, response, and recovery.

**Business continuity plan:** While SEC Rule 206(4)-4 was proposed in 2016 but never finalized, the SEC's adopting release for Rule 206(4)-7 specifically lists BCPs as an issue that "should be addressed." NASAA adopted a Model Rule requiring state-registered advisers to maintain written BCPs. The plan must cover operations during disruption, alternative office locations, data backup and recovery, client and regulator communications, key-person unavailability, and succession planning — particularly critical for one-person operations.

**Books and records (Rule 204-2):** Sixteen categories of records must be maintained for 5 years (first 2 years easily accessible), including journals, ledgers, order memoranda, communications, client agreements, advertisements, compliance records, and Form ADV. Electronic storage is permitted with proper indexing, access controls, and safeguards.

**Annual compliance review:** Must be documented in writing (per 2023 SEC amendment) and retained for 5 years. The sole principal self-conducts or engages an outside consultant for objectivity. The written report should describe each policy reviewed, findings, violations, and recommendations.

## Form ADV: the gating document for registration

Form ADV becomes required when the adviser meets the three-prong definition (advice about securities, in the business of, for compensation) and is not exempt. ERAs file only Part 1A. Fully registered advisers file Parts 1A, 2A (the "brochure"), 2B (brochure supplement), and Form CRS for retail investors.

**Part 2A (Firm Brochure) contains 18 mandatory items** covering advisory business description, fees, investment strategies and risks, disciplinary information, code of ethics, brokerage practices, custody, proxy voting, and financial information. It must be written in plain English, delivered to clients before or at signing of the advisory agreement, and updated annually within 90 days of fiscal year-end.

**Filing process and timeline:** Establish an IARD account with FINRA (~3 business days). Fund the Flex-Funding Account with filing fees. Complete Form ADV electronically. SEC has up to 45 days to declare registration effective under Section 203(c)(2). Total timeline from decision to registration: **6 weeks to 3+ months**.

**Filing costs:** SEC-registered advisers pay $225/year; state-registered advisers pay $40–$150; ERAs pay $150/year. State notice filing fees range from $40–$1,200 per state, with New York at the high end.

## Operational due diligence: what allocators actually examine

Institutional ODD has become more rigorous, with average closing time increasing from 6 to 8 months since 2022. The AIMA 2025 DDQ (Due Diligence Questionnaire) integrates technology and fund director questions into the main questionnaire, providing the industry-standard framework.

### The institutional quality checklist for sub-$10M funds

Passing full institutional ODD at sub-$10M is extremely challenging but achievable with this minimum infrastructure:

- SEC-registered RIA or Exempt Reporting Adviser with Form ADV filed
- Independent third-party fund administrator (the single most important infrastructure element)
- Independent annual audit by a reputable mid-tier firm with alternatives experience
- Prime broker with institutional-grade reporting (IBKR minimum)
- Experienced legal counsel specializing in fund formation
- Designated CCO with written compliance manual
- Written valuation policy with independent pricing (for S&P 100 stocks: Level 1 assets using exchange closing prices — the simplest and most defensible valuation category)
- Written risk management policies documenting position limits, concentration limits, and drawdown controls
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plan
- Cybersecurity policies and incident response plan
- D&O/E&O insurance
- Clean background checks on all principals
- Completed AIMA DDQ ready for investors
- Monthly investor reporting via investor portal
- Personal capital invested by manager (skin in the game)
- Operational scaling roadmap documenting growth plan

### Technology infrastructure expectations

For a systematic/AI-driven equity strategy, allocators evaluate technology posture carefully. At **$1M–$5M AUM**, a broker-provided OMS (IBKR Trader Workstation), cloud-based infrastructure, and documented automated trading processes are acceptable. At **$5M–$25M**, institutional OMS with audit trails, automated reconciliation with prime broker and administrator, real-time P&L monitoring, and compliance pre-trade checks become expected. Per Hedgeweek's 2025 survey, **45% of allocators acknowledge smaller managers now offer technology comparable to large institutions through cloud-based tools**.

**Cloud infrastructure is fully accepted** and often preferred for emerging managers — allocators evaluate security posture, not deployment model. Standard expectations include dual ISP/cellular failover for internet, UPS for power, phone-based backup trading capability with the prime broker, and data backed up to geographically separate locations.

**For AI/ML-driven systems specifically:** No SEC-specific AI regulations exist (the proposed rules were withdrawn in June 2025), but the SEC launched an AI task force in August 2025 and has made "AI-washing" (misleading claims about AI capabilities) an examination priority. Allocators expect a documented model governance framework (development lifecycle, model inventory, ownership), explainability (ability to explain model decisions — avoid "black box"), bias testing (data bias assessment, output monitoring), operational controls (kill switches, human-in-the-loop for material decisions, drift monitoring), and comprehensive audit trails of all model versions and changes.

**SOC 2 Type II audits are not typically required of the fund itself** — they're primarily for service providers. Instead, request SOC reports from service providers and implement controls aligned with SOC 2 trust service criteria internally. NIST Cybersecurity Framework serves as the appropriate documented internal standard.

## Decision tree: when to transition from solo trader to fund manager

**Continue as solo trader (Phase 1) if:**
- Live track record is less than 12 months
- Sharpe ratio is below 1.0
- Maximum drawdown exceeds 15%
- Personal capital available for the fund is under $5K
- No potential investors have expressed interest

**Form incubator fund (Phase 2a) when:**
- Live track record exceeds 12 months with Sharpe >1.0
- Personal capital available reaches $5K–$50K
- Strategy demonstrates consistent execution in live markets
- Decision to pursue fund management is firm

**Accept friends-and-family capital (Phase 2b) when:**
- Live audited track record exceeds 12–18 months
- At least $15K–$30K available for legal formation costs
- Minimum 2–3 committed investors with combined $250K+
- Legal documents (PPM, operating agreement, subscription agreement) are prepared
- ERA status filed, compliance infrastructure established

**Approach institutional seeders and allocators (Phase 3) when:**
- Live audited track record exceeds 24 months
- Sharpe ratio exceeds 1.5 with max drawdown under 10%
- AUM exceeds $1M with independent fund administrator
- Full AIMA DDQ completed and ready for review
- All ODD checklist items satisfied

**Quit the day job (Phase 4) when:**
- AUM exceeds $10M with stable, committed capital base
- Management fees alone cover fund costs plus minimum personal income
- 12–24 months of personal living expenses saved
- Track record exceeds 3 years
- Pipeline of additional potential investors identified

## Conclusion: the realistic path for Halcyon Lab

The $100-to-$3M journey is achievable but demands patience, capital efficiency, and realistic expectations about timeline and economics. **Three things matter more than anything else in the first two years: building a verified live track record with a Sharpe above 1.5, joining FundSeeder immediately as the only viable seeder platform for sub-$100K operators, and keeping costs near zero until outside capital materializes.** The biggest strategic risk is launching formal fund operations too early — before the track record justifies the $40K–$90K annual cost burden of proper infrastructure.

At the $3M base-case AUM target, the fund generates only $45K in management fees at 1.5% — creating an annual operating deficit. This means either accepting performance fee dependence (risky), subsidizing the fund from employment income (realistic during early phases), or revising the target upward to $5M–$10M where the economics genuinely work. The most capital-efficient path is trading personal capital in an incubator structure for 12–24 months while employed, then leveraging FundSeeder visibility and friends-and-family capital to reach $1M–$3M, then using that track record and AUM to access first-loss platforms or emerging manager programs that can accelerate to $5M–$10M where self-sustainability begins.
