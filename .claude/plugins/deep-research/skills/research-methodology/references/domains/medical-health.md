# Domain Preset: Medical & Health

## Description

Evidence-based medicine, health optimization, clinical practice guidelines, pharmacology, nutrition science, exercise physiology, and preventive medicine. Covers both clinical questions (diagnosis, treatment, prognosis) and personal health optimization topics. Emphasizes rigorous evidence evaluation with appropriate caveats about individual variation.

## Preferred Sources

1. **PubMed / MEDLINE** — Primary biomedical literature database
2. **Cochrane Library** — Systematic reviews, gold-standard evidence synthesis
3. **NIH guidelines** — National Institutes of Health clinical guidelines and consensus statements
4. **UpToDate** — Evidence-based clinical decision support (physician-level reference)
5. **NEJM (New England Journal of Medicine)** — High-impact clinical research
6. **The Lancet** — High-impact medical research, global health
7. **Mayo Clinic** — Patient-facing but physician-reviewed health information
8. **Examine.com** — Evidence-based supplement and nutrition analysis with study grading
9. **JAMA (Journal of the American Medical Association)** — Clinical research, guidelines
10. **WHO guidelines** — International health recommendations and evidence reviews

## Lateral Search Strategy

| Adjacent Field | Why Cross-Pollinate |
|---------------|-------------------|
| **Veterinary medicine** | Comparative physiology — animal models inform human medicine; many drugs tested in veterinary contexts first |
| **Sports science** | Performance optimization, exercise physiology, recovery science, ergogenic aids — applicable to health optimization |
| **Nutrition science** | Overlaps heavily with medical outcomes; food-as-medicine, micronutrient research, dietary pattern epidemiology |
| **Psychology / Behavioral science** | Adherence, habit formation, placebo effects, stress physiology, mind-body connection |
| **Toxicology** | Dose-response relationships, safety thresholds, interaction effects — essential for supplement and drug evaluation |

## Temporal Emphasis

Prefer recent clinical guidelines, but foundational physiology and pharmacology endure. Guidelines are updated periodically; always check for the most recent version.

- **Half-life**: 3 years
- **Foundational corpus** (always relevant):
  - Guyton & Hall, *Textbook of Medical Physiology*
  - Harrison's *Principles of Internal Medicine*
  - Goodman & Gilman's *Pharmacological Basis of Therapeutics*
  - Sackett et al., *Evidence-Based Medicine*
  - Bradford Hill criteria (causation assessment)
- **Current emphasis**: Guidelines are living documents — ACC/AHA, USPSTF, WHO, and specialty society guidelines update regularly. Check publication date and whether superseded. Nutrition and supplement evidence evolves as new RCTs publish. COVID-era research has produced both valuable evidence and low-quality rushed publications — apply extra scrutiny to 2020-2022 studies.

## Output Template Tweaks

Add the following sections to the standard report template:

### Evidence Strength (GRADE)

[Grade the overall evidence using the GRADE framework: High, Moderate, Low, Very Low. For each key recommendation, state the quality of evidence and the strength of recommendation (Strong vs Conditional). Explain what would upgrade or downgrade the evidence rating.]

### Clinical Guideline References

[Cite the most current clinical practice guidelines from relevant professional societies. Include: issuing organization, guideline title, publication year, and specific recommendation numbers. Note any disagreements between guidelines from different organizations.]

### Risk-Benefit Analysis

[Present a structured risk-benefit analysis for interventions discussed. Include: Number Needed to Treat (NNT), Number Needed to Harm (NNH), absolute risk reduction (ARR) vs relative risk reduction (RRR), effect sizes, and clinically meaningful thresholds. Avoid reporting only relative measures.]
