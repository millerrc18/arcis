# Domain Preset: Academic / Scientific

## Description

Systematic literature review, evidence synthesis, meta-analysis, and rigorous academic research methodology. This domain is meta-research — it focuses on how to find, evaluate, and synthesize scientific evidence rather than any single scientific topic. Applicable when the user needs a thorough, methodologically sound evidence review.

## Preferred Sources

1. **PubMed / MEDLINE** — Biomedical and life sciences literature
2. **Semantic Scholar** — AI-powered academic search with citation context
3. **Cochrane Library** — Gold-standard systematic reviews and meta-analyses
4. **Web of Science** — Citation indexing, impact metrics, cross-disciplinary search
5. **Google Scholar** — Broad academic search, citation tracking, related articles
6. **PRISMA guidelines** — Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
7. **Campbell Collaboration** — Systematic reviews in social sciences, education, crime/justice
8. **Scopus** — Abstract and citation database, bibliometric analysis
9. **JSTOR** — Historical and archival academic sources
10. **Preprint servers** — arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN, medRxiv (note: not peer-reviewed)

## Lateral Search Strategy

| Adjacent Field | Why Cross-Pollinate |
|---------------|-------------------|
| **Journalism / Fact-checking** — Methods for source verification, triangulation, and detecting misinformation apply directly to evidence evaluation |
| **Law / Legal reasoning** — Standards of evidence (preponderance, clear and convincing, beyond reasonable doubt) provide useful frameworks for grading research confidence |
| **Intelligence analysis** — ICD 203 analytic standards, structured analytic techniques (SATs), competing hypotheses analysis |
| **Philosophy of science** — Epistemology, falsifiability, paradigm theory, Bayesian reasoning — foundational to evaluating what counts as evidence |
| **Library and information science** — Search strategy design, controlled vocabularies, information retrieval theory |

## Temporal Emphasis

Query-dependent. Meta-analyses and systematic reviews can be old and still authoritative if no newer synthesis exists. Methodological papers retain value for decades.

- **Half-life**: 7 years
- **Foundational corpus** (always relevant):
  - Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions
  - PRISMA 2020 statement and checklist
  - GRADE framework for evidence quality assessment
  - Higgins & Green, systematic review methodology
  - Borenstein et al., *Introduction to Meta-Analysis*
  - Popper, *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*
  - Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*
- **Current emphasis**: Replication crisis awareness, pre-registration, open science practices, p-hacking and HARKing detection, living systematic reviews, AI-assisted literature screening.

## Output Template Tweaks

Add the following sections to the standard report template:

### Evidence Quality Assessment

[Grade each key piece of evidence using a structured framework (GRADE, Oxford CEBM, or similar). Assess risk of bias, consistency, directness, precision, and publication bias. Present in a summary of findings table.]

### Study Design Comparison

[Compare the study designs encountered in the literature: RCTs, cohort studies, case-control, cross-sectional, case reports, expert opinion. Note the hierarchy of evidence and what designs are available vs. what would be ideal for answering the question.]

### PRISMA-Style Search Documentation

[Document the search strategy with enough detail for reproducibility: databases searched, search terms and Boolean operators, date ranges, inclusion/exclusion criteria, number of results screened, and reasons for exclusion. Present as a PRISMA flow diagram or structured narrative.]

## Example Queries

1. "Systematic review of spaced repetition effectiveness for long-term retention"
2. "Meta-analysis methods appropriate for small-sample studies with heterogeneous populations"
3. "What is the evidence quality for intermittent fasting effects on metabolic health?"
